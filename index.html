How to adapt it to any occasion
  1. Rename your game. Try “Football Bingo,” “History Bingo,” or a title for your own gathering.
  2. Replace all 24 entries. Edit the boxes below, or open “Paste a whole list instead,” paste one word or short phrase per line, and select “Use pasted list.” The examples above are starting ideas, not complete lists.
  3. Agree on what counts. Choose who or what to listen to and whether word variations count. Keep the FREE center.
  4. Generate, print, and play. Choose your board count and optional player names, select Generate, then print one or two boards per page. Five in a row, column, or diagonal wins.

For a classroom review variation, read clues or definitions and have students mark the matching terms. You can also adapt the game for a book club, reunion, meeting, or movie night.

Everyone gets the same 24 entries in different positions. Choose mostly words likely to be heard. Export your game to save or share your custom setup.

A tip for a lively game
Choose mostly words likely to come up. More boards can mean an earlier winner, but enough words still need to be said. Agree whether deliberately prompting a word is allowed. Ties share the win.
